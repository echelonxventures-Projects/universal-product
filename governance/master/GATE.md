# LXM assimilation gate — implemented behaviour

Scope: requirement, decision and evidence contributions (`kind: requirement`, `decision`, `evidence`). Everything here is implemented in `assimilate.py` and exercised by `test_assimilate.py` (38 scenarios). Anything not listed here is not implemented.

## Files

| File | Role | Canonical? |
|---|---|---|
| `reqs.txt`, `stages.txt`, `sources.txt`, `coverage.txt`, `decisions.txt`, `evidence.txt`, `trace.txt`, `frame.md` | Canonical inputs. `decisions.txt` is the decision register, rendered as Part C; `trace.txt` is implementation traceability, rendered as Part H.4 | Yes |
| `sources/<SRC>.extract.md`, `.clauses`, `.provenance.md` | Source text, clause ledger, provenance | Yes (part of the state digest) |
| `out/LXM-MASTER.md`, `out/LXM-RECONCILIATION.md` | Generated by `build.py`; never edited | Derived |
| `lexicon.txt` | Matching vocabulary (synonyms, stop words, negation) | Gate configuration |
| `gate.json` | Paths and hashes of the external sources the validator checks | Gate configuration |
| `config_adapter.json` | The configuration adapter's declarations; the validator checks trace links against them | Gate configuration |
| `intake/exceptions.txt` | LXM-EXC entries for refused contributions (QUARANTINED / REJECTED / RESOLVED) | No: intake log |
| `intake/ledger.jsonl` | One JSON line per gate run, every outcome | No: intake log |

Canonical state is hashed by `state_digest()` over the canonical inputs, `sources/` and the derived files.

## Pipeline

1. **Receive.** Parse `key: value` fields. The contribution's SHA-256 is its identity in the ledger and the exception log. `section` may list several clauses of one source (`§2.8,§2.9`); every relation the gate writes then names each clause separately.
2. **Check.** Required fields; no unknown fields; kind; text length; source registered in `sources.txt`; scope registered (`scopes.txt`, or a stage of `stages.txt`; no fixed list in code); every referenced record exists; `applied-in` names an existing stage action by its stable ID (`STG-NN.A-k`; the legacy `STG-NN #k` means the same ID, never a position); a supersession has a reason. Failure → REJECTED.
3. **Owner holds and decision authority.**
   - An open QUARANTINED exception whose reason is *owner-reserved* binds its source clause. Any later contribution citing that clause is refused, however it is phrased, unless it is authorized.
   - `decision:` authorizes only an **adopted** decision in the register (`decisions.txt`) whose `a:` field records a scope (source clauses, and optionally records). Every clause the contribution cites must be in that scope. The register is the only authority: prose in `frame.md` that resembles a decision row grants nothing.
   - The record it changes must also be in scope, or be a record created under the decision (it carries the decision in `k:`).
   - Open (C.2), unrecorded and unscoped decisions authorize nothing. K1–K8 (C.1) have an explicit empty scope (`None`), confirmed by the owner. They stay adopted and binding, and the records that carry them still refuse change, but citing them authorizes nothing.
   - A cited decision must authorize the change itself, whatever the target. If a contribution cites a decision outside its scope, the gate refuses it (REJECTED), including for a new record, an ordinary record or a recognition. Otherwise the gate would record the decision on content it never decided.
   - An authorized change records the decision on what it changed (`k:`), once.
4. **Discover.** Every record in `reqs.txt` is indexed, from all scopes; nothing is pre-filtered.
5. **Classify** each sentence of the text against every record:
   - EXACT: normalized clause is a substring of a record.
   - CONFLICT: shares ≥ 0.45 of its meaning with a record sentence, and ≥ 3 shared terms asserted on one side are negated on the other (negation scoped to its phrase; conditions such as "if no … exists" ignored), or numbers differ at ≥ 0.6 shared meaning.
   - COVERED: ≥ 0.8 of the clause's terms are in one record, or in two records that each contribute ≥ 2 terms the other lacks (composition).
   - RELATED: 0.45 to 0.8. NOVEL: below 0.45.
   - Governed matching rules (`lexicon.txt` and `Lexicon`):
     - `@frame:` list lead-ins (e.g. "All capabilities must conform to") are removed before matching, because the list item carries the meaning.
     - A double negation ("no X without Y", "never X unless Y") states an obligation, not a prohibition. Two plain negations ("no X and not Y") are not a double negation.
     - A short negated subject carries its negation through the commas of its sentence ("No configuration, valid or invalid, may bypass …").
     - A negation contradiction needs the same shared meaning (0.6) as a numeric one; below that, shared nouns alone are not a contradiction.
     - Hyphenated identifiers (`amazon-62`, `PR-053`) are identifiers, never quantities.
     - A calendar date and a file path are identifiers, not quantities: `GAPS-2026-10-31.md` holds no numbers that could contradict "one by one". (Adopted after `gate_impact.py` showed exactly one classification change, the false contradiction it removes.)
     - Clause identifiers are open-ended (`§7.1`, `§A.3`, `§2.2.10`): any letters and digits, never a numeric-only format.
6. **Resolve** to the minimum mutation:

| Outcome | When | Mutation |
|---|---|---|
| RECOGNIZE-EXACT / -SEMANTIC | every clause EXACT/COVERED by one record | lineage + coverage relation on that record |
| COMPOSE | covered, by two or more records | lineage + coverage on each |
| EXTEND / PARTIAL→EXTEND | `extends:` given; uncovered clauses appended | text appended, lineage, `extends` relation |
| NEW / PARTIAL→NEW | uncovered clauses all NOVEL; scope, title, `applied-in` given | new record (next ID) with only the uncovered clauses, cited in the named stage action |
| SUPERSEDE | `supersedes:` + reason, owner authority | text replaced, version +1, prior text kept as `h:` history |
| NOOP | nothing left to change | none (idempotent replay) |
| QUARANTINED | contradiction, ambiguity (RELATED with no resolution), owner-decided record, owner hold | none; LXM-EXC written |
| REJECTED | intake check, insufficient authority, unsupported `recognized-as`, or staged validation fails | none; LXM-EXC written |
| ERROR | the gate itself fails (e.g. the staged build) | none; ledger only, no LXM-EXC (the contribution was not judged) |

   **Resolutions** a contributor can declare:
   - `extends: <record>` adds the clause to an existing record.
   - `recognized-as: <record>` claims the record already expresses the clause. The claim is accepted only where the clause is at least RELATED to that record; otherwise REJECTED.
   - `distinct-from: <records>` confirms the meaning is new.
   - `relates: <record> supports|depends-on` records a relationship without coverage.

   **Authority** (and decision scope, step 3):
   - `historical` sources can only recognize.
   - Changing a record whose lineage cites an `owner-decision` source, or that carries an LXM-DEC reference, needs `decision:`.
7. **Validate.** The mutation is applied to a staging copy, which also holds `sources/` and `intake/`. `build.py` regenerates the copy and the full `validate.py` runs on it. The gate then adds its own checks: every clause is now expressed (a supported declared recognition counts), and no changed record duplicates another. Any failure → REJECTED, root untouched.
8. **Assimilate.** Staged files are copied beside their targets as `*.staged`; the journal `.assimilate-journal` is written and fsynced; each file is then replaced with `os.replace`. Every gate run first calls `recover()`, which completes a journal left by an interrupted commit (roll-forward).
9. **Propagate.** The committed derived files are the ones generated in staging. Any later rebuild reproduces them byte-for-byte; the fixtures check this after every acceptance.
10. **Record.** An accepted resubmission closes the exceptions it was blocked by inside the same staged commit, adding a `resolved:` line that names the outcome, the contribution, the records and the decision, so the generated Master never shows a stale hold for it. Replay is idempotent even after later changes. **Replay by identity:** a contribution already accepted (same ID and SHA-256 in the ledger), whose recorded relations still hold, is NOOP however matching has shifted since, because records grow. Within a run, a supersession whose history entry already exists is NOOP and never reverts a later version, and a clause the record already extends is not re-recorded as covered. Exceptions for the same contribution ID and SHA are reused, not duplicated. An accepted or NOOP resubmission under the same ID marks its open exceptions RESOLVED. A refusal or error that changed canonical state would stop the gate (`INVARIANT BROKEN`); none does. Every run appends a ledger line with these fields:
   - `seq`, time and gate version;
   - contribution ID, SHA-256 and path;
   - source, section, authority and text status;
   - outcome, classification, clauses and changed records;
   - exception ID, reason and validation summary;
   - state digest before and after.

## Decision contributions (`kind: decision`)

They run through the same staging, build, validation, journaled commit, exception log and ledger as requirements.
- **`action: open`** records a new question under the next decision ID. It decides nothing, so it needs no approval.
- **`action: adopt`** records the owner's adoption of an open decision. It needs:
  - the outcome, the effect, the scope (`authorizes`: `None`, or source clauses and records), who adopted it, the provenance and the rationale;
  - the approval stored verbatim under `intake/decisions/`, with its SHA-256 in `approval-sha256`. The approval must name the decision.

  The gate checks that the file exists, matches its hash and names the decision. It cannot verify who wrote it.
- **An adopted decision is never edited.** A changed adoption of the same ID is QUARANTINED as owner-reserved. Resubmitting the identical adoption is NOOP.
- **Supersession is not implemented yet.** It stays a direct, owner-approved edit, checked by `validate.py`.
- **Applied or pending.** A decision with a non-empty scope is applied by later gated changes inside its scope. Until the first one, the validator reports it as pending. A decision with an empty scope must already be carried by a record.

## Evidence contributions (`kind: evidence`)

The canonical home is `evidence.txt`, rendered as Part G.3.
- **`action: add`** records an observation under the next ID. It needs the fact, the source (where it was observed), the method, the time, the observer, and `verifies` (the requirements, decisions or evidence it is evidence for). Every reference in `verifies` must exist.
- **`action: supersede`** records a newer observation. The entry it replaces changes its status to "superseded by …" and nothing else.
- **Evidence is never edited.** A changed entry under an existing ID is REJECTED. Resubmitting the same observation is NOOP.

## The log (`intake/ledger.jsonl`, schema 2)

- **Hash chain.** Each line carries `prev`, the SHA-256 of the line before it. `validate.py` and `verify_chain()` reject a broken chain, so an edited, removed or reordered line is detected. The 559 lines before the chain started are accepted as its legacy start.
- **Recorded mutation.** Each accepted line records `mutation`:
  - for requirements, the exact operations;
  - for decisions and evidence, the resulting text;
  - for every changed file, its SHA-256.

  `project.py` rebuilds the canonical registers from a checkpoint by applying those mutations in order, without judging anything again. Level 2 is a projection of the log (design §1).

## Every canonical kind goes through the gate

| Kind | Canonical file | Changes the gate accepts |
|---|---|---|
| `requirement` | `reqs.txt`, `stages.txt` | recognize, compose, extend, new, supersede (above) |
| `decision` | `decisions.txt` | open; adopt with the owner's approval on file |
| `evidence` | `evidence.txt` | add; supersede by a newer observation |
| `trace` | `trace.txt` | add or replace a requirement's links, with their basis |
| `scope` | `scopes.txt` | register a scope at a stated position |
| `source` | `sources.txt` and `sources/` | register the next source; its extract, provenance and clause ledger come in through the same change (`files-from`) |
| `stage-action` | `stages.txt` | add an action with the next permanent ID, citing requirements |
| `prose` | `frame.md` | replace a section's body, or insert after an anchor; owner sections need an adopted decision |

- **No direct edits.** `state_matches_log()`: the canonical state must equal the state the last gate run left. CI fails otherwise.
- **Baseline.** `assimilate.py <root> --baseline <reason>` records a chained baseline once. It needs a full validation pass and a reason, and monitoring lists every baseline. The first, at the start of enforcement on 2026-10-31, covers the direct edits made before every kind had an adapter.
- **Re-validation at change time.** A staged change that leaves any recorded source clause unexpressed (`revalidate.py`, the records covering a clause taken together) is REJECTED.
- **Capture.** `capture.py` is the way in for any file or transcript message. Secrets are refused, and sensitivity is recorded in the provenance. The files wait in `intake/<id>/files/` until the gate registers the source.
- **Feedback.** `proposals.py` turns Level 3 outcomes into write-once proposals (`intake/proposals/`) and an evidence contribution, never edits.
- **Small files.** `shard.py prove` splits the registers into one file per record with an index, and rebuilds the Master byte-identical. This is a prototype; the layout changes with the kernel move.

## Governing the gate itself

- `gate_impact.py` classifies every contribution under the current gate and under a proposed one, and reports each change before the proposal is adopted.
- `gate_accuracy.py` measures the gate against `gate_reference.txt`. Today 9 of 11 cases are correct; the 2 errors are the held false contradictions. CI allows no more than 2.
- `knowledge_status.py` reports the backlog, the held queue (resolver and age from `knowledge.json`), review lapses, log integrity and gate accuracy.
- `states.py` computes each requirement's lifecycle state from evidence (Level 3).

## Known limits (true of the code today)

- **Matching is lexical, not semantic understanding.** Terminology the lexicon does not know is unmatched. A clause can still reach 0.8 while missing one material word, which is why source coverage also gets a recorded human or agent review: see the SRC-0008 R1/R2 supersessions.
- **H.3 shows holds as of the last generation.** A refusal does not regenerate the Master (that would change canonical state), so a new hold appears in H.3 at the next accepted change or rebuild.
- **Clause ledgers** assert completeness only when their status is `complete`; while `in-progress`, uncovered clauses are listed but block nothing. Moving a ledger to `complete` is a direct edit, checked by `validate.py`.
- **Gated edits:** requirement records and the opening and adoption of decisions are gated. Edits to `sources.txt`, `frame.md`, `stages.txt`, `trace.txt` and the tools, and decision supersession, are made directly and checked by `validate.py` afterwards.
