# DECISION 2 — UNIVERSAL MULTI-BUSINESS, MULTI-INDUSTRY PLATFORM

## 1. Owner's Decision

LUXORA shall be built as a universal, configuration-driven business platform capable of configuring, operating and extending multiple types of businesses, platforms, operating models, industries and domains through a common platform foundation.

Amazon-like e-commerce is the first production deployment configuration. It is not the platform's architectural boundary, exclusive business model or long-term capability limit.

The platform must be built to support a broad and extensible ecosystem of business models, including but not limited to:

- E-commerce marketplaces and multi-vendor commerce.
- Mobility, ride-hailing, delivery and on-demand services.
- Fintech, payments, wallets and financial-service workflows.
- Consultancy, professional services and service marketplaces.
- Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP).
- Customer Relationship Management (CRM).
- Retail, wholesale, distribution and B2B commerce.
- Subscription, membership, booking and appointment businesses.
- Logistics, transportation, fulfilment and supply-chain operations.
- Education, healthcare, travel, hospitality and other industry-specific models.
- Future business models, industries and domain combinations that do not yet exist.

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive or a hardcoded limit.

## 2. Build the Platform, Not Just an Extensible Architecture

LUXORA must implement the reusable mechanisms and domain capabilities required to configure supported business models. It must not merely document that future engineering could add them.

The common platform should provide, as applicable:

- Discoverable business-model, industry, domain, capability, service and component registries.
- Reusable identity, organization, tenant, access-control, catalog, commerce, workflow, payment, wallet, financial, notification, analytics, governance and audit capabilities.
- Configurable business-model definitions and versioned deployment profiles.
- Composition and dependency management for domain capabilities.
- Configurable entities, relationships, attributes, schemas, lifecycle states, workflows, business policies, permissions and user experiences.
- Standard interfaces and extension contracts for industry-specific capabilities and external integrations.
- Configuration validation, simulation, dependency checks, approval, versioning, activation, observability and rollback.
- Isolation and governance between tenants, businesses, industries and configurations.
- A canonical source of truth for every capability and its configuration.
- A governed mechanism for registering new capabilities and making them discoverable.

These mechanisms must be implemented using the existing canonical platform architecture wherever possible. Do not create parallel engines, duplicate registries or competing sources of truth.

## 3. Configure Multiple Business Platforms From the Same Foundation

A new business deployment should be created by selecting and composing the required supported capabilities, business-model definition, configuration profile, rules, integrations, permissions, workflows and presentation.

The design must avoid requiring a separate copy or fork of the LUXORA core for every business type.

For example:

- An Amazon-like marketplace configuration composes product discovery, seller management, offers, carts, checkout, payments, orders, fulfilment, returns and settlements.
- An Uber-like mobility configuration composes service discovery, driver and vehicle management, availability, matching, dispatch, trip lifecycle, location integration, pricing, payments and safety-related workflows.
- A fintech configuration composes the relevant customer and account models, payment or ledger capabilities, financial controls, reconciliation, risk policies and regulatory integrations.
- An ERP configuration composes the appropriate finance, inventory, procurement, sales, fulfilment and operational workflows.
- A CRM configuration composes customer and relationship records, interaction history, pipeline workflows, permissions, tasks, reporting and relevant integrations.

These examples define the intended composition model. Each capability must be implemented and registered before it can be claimed as available.

A business profile must be able to select supported capabilities without modifying unrelated platform code. Where a genuinely new computation, protocol or domain behavior is required, implement it through the governed extension mechanism and register it for reuse.

## 4. Existing and Future Business Models

LUXORA must not impose a fixed enumeration of business types, industry types, domain types or capability combinations.

Business models, industry classifications, domain definitions and capability compositions must be discoverable and extensible through governed registries and schemas. New combinations of existing capabilities should be configurable without modifying the platform core.

Future capabilities requiring new executable behavior must be implementable as modular extensions, then registered, validated, tested and made available through the same platform mechanisms.

Avoid promises of literal infinite implementation. The requirement is an extensible architecture and configuration system without artificial business-model limits, unnecessary core changes or hardcoded enumerations that prevent legitimate expansion.

## 5. Initial Production Configuration

The first production deployment shall be the Amazon-like marketplace.

However, marketplace-first must not cause the underlying platform to become a marketplace-only implementation. Foundational abstractions and reusable capabilities required by the wider platform must be developed correctly at the outset, rather than replaced with marketplace-specific assumptions.

Prioritize the work needed for the first production release while implementing the reusable platform foundations and capabilities required by the approved universal architecture.

## 6. Capability Availability and Truthful Status

Maintain a canonical capability registry and traceability matrix distinguishing at least:

1. Defined: the capability and its contract are specified.
2. Implemented: executable functionality exists.
3. Registered: the platform can discover the capability.
4. Configurable: authorized users can configure it through supported interfaces.
5. Integrated: its declared dependencies and interfaces work together.
6. Tested: applicable functional, integration, security and regression evidence exists.
7. Certified for a business profile: the composed configuration has passed its defined acceptance criteria.
8. Production-enabled: the exact profile is approved and ready for its target environment.

A capability must not be represented as configurable merely because a schema, placeholder, roadmap item or registry record exists. The platform must actually consume and enforce the configuration.

Likewise, a business profile must not be described as production-ready merely because its component capabilities exist independently.

## 7. Required Acceptance Evidence

Engineering must inspect the actual repository and produce a capability matrix identifying:

- Business models and industry domains already implemented.
- Reusable capabilities shared across those business models.
- Capabilities that are genuinely configurable today.
- Capabilities that exist but require additional integration or testing.
- Capabilities that remain unimplemented.
- Hardcoded domain assumptions and unnecessary coupling.
- Dependencies, migration needs, security constraints and missing test coverage.
- The minimum complete Amazon-like marketplace production configuration.
- The next business configurations that can be assembled using existing capabilities without new core changes.

Demonstrate configuration of the first marketplace and representative non-marketplace profiles using the shared platform foundation. Test that changing one tenant's or business profile's configuration does not unintentionally alter another profile.

**Final acceptance principle:** LUXORA is a universal multi-business platform whose supported business models can be configured from shared, implemented and discoverable capabilities. The first production deployment is an Amazon-like marketplace, while the platform is built from the outset to configure and extend additional business models and industries through the same governed mechanisms.
