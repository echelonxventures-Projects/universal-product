Decision 1 — Universal monetary configuration

Monetary precision, rounding modes, tax calculations, discount allocation, currency support, fees and settlement policies must be defined through governed, versioned configuration rather than hardcoded business assumptions.

Required safeguards: exact monetary representation, explicit currency, deterministic calculations, validation, audit history and reproducible results.



Decision 2 — Marketplace first, universal business architecture

The first production release will serve an Amazon-like e-commerce marketplace. The underlying architecture must be designed to support other business models and industries, including Uber-like mobility, fintech, consultancy, ERP, CRM and future business categories.

Important distinction: the architecture must be extensible across these domains, but each business capability must still be implemented, configured, tested and certified before being represented as production-ready.



Decision 3 — Configurable business rules

Existing and future business rules must be managed through a universal, extensible rule and policy mechanism. This includes taxes, promotions, coupons, refunds, eligibility, pricing, fees, commissions, settlement and other applicable rules.

Rules need definitions, schemas, validation, effective dates, precedence, versioning, simulation, approval, auditability and controlled activation.



Decision 4 — Configurable exception and data-resolution policies

Legacy monetary records, ambiguous conversions, rounding exceptions and refund-allocation cases must be handled through explicit, configurable policies—not undocumented assumptions.

Configuration must never bypass financial invariants. Ambiguous financial records must be preserved and quarantined for resolution rather than silently altered.



Decision 5 — Universal payment and wallet integration

The platform must support configurable payment integrations, with initial Indian-market requirements including Razorpay, PhonePe, UPI and platform wallets. The architecture must accommodate additional payment gateways, processors, banks, payment methods, currencies and future payment technologies.

Required safeguards: a provider-neutral integration contract, capability discovery, credential and secret isolation, transaction idempotency, signed webhook verification, reconciliation, failover policies and provider-specific certification.
